# Demeter automation design

The first build is an indoor, recirculating tower with three 160 mm tiers, nine rooted lettuce plugs, a 15 L working nutrient reservoir, and a separate 61 L freshwater tank. An ESP32 runs irrigation, lighting, freshwater top-off, nutrient replenishment, and pH correction locally. All nine cups share one editable crop profile per batch.

The operating goal is 30 days between planting and harvest with the tanks prepared at the start. The first planted run establishes water use, crop spacing, probe drift, and whether a solution change is needed. Pump flow, dosing limits, and light levels below are commissioning targets.

## Tower and plumbing

```text
Freshwater tank ── top-off pump ── air gap ───────────────┐
Nutrients A and B, pH Up and Down ── four separate lines ─┤
                                                         ▼
15 L working reservoir ── screen ── circulation pump ──┬── mixing return
        ▲                                               └── flow meter
        │                                                       │
        │                               short 1/2 in ID hose to flange
        │                                                       │
        │                               sealed Ø10 mm riser up to lid
        │                                                       ▼
        └──────────── bottom return ◀────── three rain trays in cascade
```

- Print three grow modules, three rain trays and nine cups from `Demeter-sealed-riser.sc`, plus one top cap and flange. Each module has three Ø49 mm cup sockets tilted 45° upward and spaced 120° apart. The existing Ø48 mm net-cup body has 0.5 mm nominal radial clearance. Rotate the middle tier 60° to stagger the plants. Same-side plants are 320 mm apart with this arrangement.
- Clamp a short 1/2 in ID flexible hose to the Ø13.3 mm plain tail beneath the reservoir flange. The flange and every module have a Ø10 mm coaxial supply bore. The centered lid inlet feeds four Ø6 mm downward outlets over the top tray. The pump, bypass, and hose stay by the reservoir. The open return paths around the riser drain into the reservoir by gravity.
- Seat the 7 mm lower tongue in the next part's counterbore. The joint has 0.25 mm nominal radial clearance and a Ø104 mm return bore across the seam. The load-bearing shoulder sits within the thickened outer wall. The rain tray rests on a small shoulder on the center riser, with a 3 mm radial gap at the tower wall. Its 18 Ø3.2 mm holes on a Ø90 mm circle spread rain around each tier. During excess inflow, the lower outer rim spills into the wall gap.
- Fit one 13 mm ID × 2 mm water-contact EPDM O-ring at each riser joint, including the flange and cap. A three-tier stack uses four rings. Each mating face has a 0.8 mm deep annular groove, Ø12.6 mm inside and Ø17.8 mm outside. With the faces seated, the two halves give 1.6 mm gland depth and 20% nominal squeeze. Check finished depth and flatness on the first print. Use washers and two external M5 tie rods through the matching cap and flange holes. Place their axes 30° away from the cup axes so they clear both stagger positions. Draw the stack down evenly until the joint lands seat, and verify the seals under running pump pressure before planting. [Static face seal gland design](https://www.parker.com/content/dam/Parker-com/Literature/O-Ring-Division-Literature/O-Ring-ehandbook-pdfs/Design-chart-4-3-for-O-ring-face-seal-glands.pdf)
- Start at **0.8–1.2 L/min measured on the supply line after the bypass**. Level the tower, pre-wet the trays and confirm each plug receives water through the perforations. Add a short wick inside a cup if a starter plug sits clear of the wetted slots. The initial static lift estimate is 0.7–0.9 m. Add filter, meter, tubing, manifold and valve losses when checking the pump curve. Confirm headspace after pump-off drainback at the highest usable setting.
- Run low-flow irrigation continuously at first, including overnight. Fresh plugs need reliable contact with water until roots extend. Support a timed duty cycle in the crop profile once a full wet/dry test establishes a safe off period. Keep a low-flow return bypass for mixing and surface agitation. During timed irrigation, the controller starts circulation and proves flow before taking dosing readings or adding solution.
- Use a large, removable root guard over the pump intake, keep the tray rims and wall gaps clear, and route all joints over containment. Start with compact loose-leaf varieties and check that mature roots leave the tray outlets and overflow paths clear.
- Use a rigid frame to restrain the tower and carry the lights. The Ø160 mm flange has four Ø5.5 mm holes on a Ø136 mm bolt circle. Two opposite holes line up with the cap tie rods. Reinforce the pail lid around the flange. Use a removable, light-blocking probe rack off to the side of the flange. Locate pH, EC and water temperature below the lowest permitted running water level, away from bubbles and dosing outlets. Keep the connectors dry and allow the probes to lift out for calibration.
- Place the vented refill tank low with all feed ends above its full waterline and above the receiving tank's maximum level. Give each stock line the same physical air break above both liquid levels. Mount dosing heads above their stock bottles and catch any leaks below them. Size a shared catch basin for the installed liquid inventory, including pump-off drainback, with a leak sensor at the lowest dry point.

### Refill sizing

Allow at least **53 L of usable freshwater** for nine plants using a planning allowance of 0.15 L per plant per day, 30 days, and a 30% reserve. The 61 L nominal tank leaves room for pickup depth and vent headspace. Replace the estimate with logged draw at near-full canopy and include evaporation from the actual rig. For 12 cups, the same assumptions require about **70 L usable**. The reservoir's 15 L working volume is kept in reserve for circulation and mixing. [Published lettuce water use for a different hydroponic design](https://www.ctahr.hawaii.edu/hawaii/downloads/three_non-circulating_hydroponic_methods_for_growing_lettuce.pdf)

## Wiring and independent cutoffs

Use a GFCI-protected outlet. Keep the packaged AC adapters and light relay dry, outside containment. Run fused, low-voltage wiring around the tower. Mount the dry electronics above splash level with downward cable entries. Use DC-rated switches and contacts, measure startup current, size each fuse to protect its wiring, and suppress inductive transients per the device datasheets. [GFCI guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/099_0.pdf)

```text
24 V supply ── fused distribution ─┬── circulation cutout ── pump driver ── pump
                                   ├── fill cutout ── 12 V buck ── top-off driver
                                   ├── chemical cutout ── fixed-time motor permit
                                   │                     └── one-of-four motor feeds
                                   ├── fan driver
                                   └── 5 V buck ── ESP32, sensors and status
ESP32 ── low-voltage trigger ── normally-off relay outlet ── packaged light kit
```

- Wire independent, opening-on-fault switches in energize-to-run permit circuits. A leak or stop drops all wet actuator power and requires a local reset. Low-low opens irrigation and chemical power. High-high opens fill and chemical power. The refill tank low switch inhibits only fill. Size contacts for the loads or use them to drive interlock relays. Feed auxiliary contact status back to the controller so wire breaks and active cutoffs become alarms. Set the normal full mark and high-high using the measured pump-off drainback volume.
- Keep chemical pump logic on 3.3 V and switch the motor supply independently. Latch the one-of-four motor selection for the duration of each permitted shot. Use an independent, non-retriggering pulse cutoff that removes motor power if the controller holds the request on, plus an external watchdog that drops fill and chemical permits if the controller stops responding. Configure the chemical motors to start idle. Size the timeout after measuring the single largest permitted shot. Use a separate independent fill run limit. For unattended testing, physically limit each pH bottle's working inventory to an amount shown by a lowest-volume titration to stay within the commissioning stop bounds if released.
- Use a short 3.3 V I²C bus inside the dry enclosure. Assign pump addresses `0x30` through `0x33` one at a time, leaving the DS3231 at `0x68`. Power open-collector flow meters from their rated supply and pull up their pulse outputs to 3.3 V at the controller. Keep motor wiring apart from probe cables. Use protected inputs for long wires and check pH/EC readings while each actuator switches.

| ESP32-S3 assignment | Connection |
| --- | --- |
| GPIO 4 / 5 | I²C SDA / SCL |
| GPIO 6 | Top-feed flow pulse input |
| GPIO 12 / 13 / 14 / 15 | Irrigation / top-off / light / fan commands |
| GPIO 16 / 17–18 / 21 | Dose-window trigger / hardware one-of-four selector / watchdog heartbeat |
| MCP23017 at `0x20` | Level and interlock feedback, local service input, buzzer/status |

All actuator commands need external default-off bias. The controller may stay powered through an interlock so it can log and report the fault. Power the DevKit from one documented input at a time during bench programming. [ESP32 power input options](https://documentation.espressif.com/api/resource/path/docs/projects/esp-dev-kits/en/latest/esp32s3/esp32-s3-devkitc-1/user_guide_v1.1.html)

## Firmware behavior

1. **Boot and maintain irrigation.** Start with outputs off. Validate interlocks, retrieve the active profile and check the RTC. Resume irrigation when the reservoir is safe. Verify supply flow after priming. Low or absent top-feed flow inhibits dosing and raises an alarm. Complete flow loss stops the pump after one controlled re-prime attempt. Individual blocked tray holes can leave the top-feed reading normal. Check the trays and plug wetting during commissioning. RTC or network failure leaves safe irrigation available. RTC failure inhibits scheduled lighting and chemical dosing until time is valid.
2. **Refill and settle.** Debounce the normal low request and top off with plain source water until the full switch changes state or the calibrated time/volume ceiling trips. Check for inconsistent levels and unexpectedly frequent refills. Prevent dosing during fill and allow the solution to mix before interpreting pH or EC. A stuck refill output must still stop at the independent high-high or fill timer.
3. **Restore EC, then pH.** Use recent stable, temperature-compensated samples after proven circulation. If EC remains below the crop band, dispense a small portion of A, mix, then dispense its paired B portion and mix again. Use the product's A:B ratio and measured pump outputs. If pH is persistently below the band, use pH Up. If it is persistently above the band, use pH Down. Dose one measured shot, mix for the observed settling time, then resample. Require a confirmed post-dose response and latch dosing off on no response, a reversed response or an overshoot that would otherwise trigger the other reagent. High EC after top-off calls for inspection or a solution change. EC tracks total conductivity and can miss a nutrient imbalance. [EC and pH in hydroponic systems](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/electrical-conductivity-and-ph-guide-for-hydroponics)
4. **Limit each dose.** Derive the maximum shot and rolling 24-hour limit from calibration and titration with the actual water, nutrient solution, stock, and minimum operating volume. Persist the reserved quantity before enabling a motor, then record the result. Count an interrupted shot against the budget and latch chemical dosing for inspection if completion is unknown. Keep auto-dosing disabled until all limits and calibration checks pass. Prime each line to a waste cup in a timed service mode. Store pump calibrations and safety limits independently of crop profiles.
5. **Run the light schedule and report faults.** Use the local RTC to run the profile photoperiod, including after a network outage or power cycle. Keep fan circulation available during lights-off if needed. Log once per minute and on state changes. Include pH, EC, air/water temperatures, humidity, top-feed flow, level changes, refill/dose amounts, and fault cause. Estimate stock remaining from initial fill minus reserved dose volumes. Show persistent faults locally and send alerts for leaks, loss of flow, exhausted water or stock budget, out-of-range temperature or chemistry, and a missing controller heartbeat when remote monitoring is available. A dry-side UPS can keep circulation and the controller running long enough to cover a tested short outage.

### Editable crop profiles

Provide a local setup and status page with save, duplicate, export, and activate controls. Keep one active recipe with optional stages by day since transplant. Validate units, ordered bands, transition dates and a nonzero daily dark period. Keep runtime dose and refill counters across restarts and recipe changes. Stage fields include pH and EC bands, light hours and DLI target, irrigation mode and on/off times, and air/water temperature alerts. An intensity setting is recorded from the light controller used during commissioning. Use one shared recipe for crops with compatible requirements.

| Setting | Initial loose-leaf lettuce profile |
| --- | --- |
| Start | Healthy rooted plugs in all nine net cups |
| pH | 5.6–6.0, with a 5.8 center target. Enable pH Up below the lower edge and pH Down above the upper edge after stable repeat readings. |
| EC at 25 °C | 1.2–1.6 mS/cm total with low-EC source water and a compatible nutrient product. Start near the lower end for small transplants. Adjust for measured source EC, alkalinity and the selected product. |
| Lights | 06:00–22:00 local time. Start around 210–280 µmol/m²/s at each canopy, equivalent to about 12–16 mol/m²/day at 16 hours. Confirm with a PAR meter and adjust distance or intensity. |
| Irrigation | Continuous at 0.8–1.2 L/min to the top tray as an initial test range, tuned to keep every plug wet and draining. |
| Temperature and humidity | Start around 18–22 °C water, 19–24 °C air, and 50–70% RH. Investigate sustained water above 25 °C. |

These are prototype settings. Cornell's lettuce program provides the starting pH band and a reference for lighting and crop time. The EC range here is expressed as total solution EC. [Cornell CEA lettuce handbook](https://cea.cals.cornell.edu/files/2019/06/Cornell-CEA-Lettuce-Handbook-.pdf), [Oklahoma State crop ranges and source-water guidance](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/electrical-conductivity-and-ph-guide-for-hydroponics)

## Commissioning before a month unattended

1. Fit-test two staggered tiers, check groove depth and alignment, and verify the riser joints with plain water while the tie rods are snug. Test all four lid outlets and examine the printed parts for seepage. Reinforce the reservoir lid and run the assembled tower for at least 72 hours. Calibrate the flow meter and top-off pump. Check that all nine plugs wet and drain at both level limits, especially on the bottom tier. Measure pump-off drainback. Temporarily cover the perimeter holes on a tray to check the rim overflow at maximum commissioned flow. Check the complete supply failure alarm independently.
2. Trigger high-high, low-low, an empty refill tank, a leak, disconnected/implausible probes, a held command, a reboot during a mock dose, and Wi-Fi loss. Verify actual pump power at the load during interlock tests. Check that lights return on schedule and every fault creates the expected local indication and event log. Measure light output at all nine cup positions and monitor water heating with lights and pumps running.
3. Calibrate the chemistry probes and dose pumps, then titrate separate samples of the prepared nutrient solution at minimum and normal operating volumes. Scale the result conservatively, measure actual mixing time with the bypass running, and confirm small shots in the full reservoir with a handheld meter. Save the validated limits and replace the mock-dose fluid with the correctly labeled stock.
4. Run one supervised crop through harvest. Record mature canopy freshwater use, the total draw from each stock, probe drift, highest water temperature, plant growth and root buildup. Size the A/B stock to the observed draw plus reserve and check whether the permitted pH inventories cover the same interval. Verify that the crop reaches harvest without a mid-cycle intervention before treating 30 days as an operating interval. Clean the wetted path between batches and start each batch with fresh solution and calibrated probes.

The SolidCraft file contains the three-cup module with an integral riser, the net cup, a distribution lid, a hose-fed flange and the center-mounted rain tray. The printed seals and water distribution through a staggered stack still need bench validation.
